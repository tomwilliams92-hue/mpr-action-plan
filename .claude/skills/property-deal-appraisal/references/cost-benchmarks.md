# Default cost assumptions

Every figure here is a starting point, not a quote. Use them to fill gaps so an
appraisal can run, but tell the user which defaults you applied — an appraisal
that silently assumes a £1,500 buyer's premium when the real one is £6,600 is
worse than no appraisal.

Ranges reflect the North Wales / Merseyside market at the time of writing.
Assume all figures include VAT unless noted; VAT on fees is a real cost to an
SPV unless it is VAT-registered, which most BTL companies are not because
residential letting is exempt.

## Auction fees — the ones that catch people out

The single biggest difference between a guide price and what you actually hand
over. Establish which kind of auction it is before anything else.

**Traditional auction** (contracts exchange on the fall of the hammer, 28 days to
complete):

| Item | Typical |
| --- | ---: |
| Buyer's premium | £1,500 – £3,000 |
| Auction admin / documentation fee | £300 – £600 |
| Seller's legal costs and search pack passed to buyer | £1,000 – £1,500 |

**Modern method of auction** (a reservation agreement, 56 days, increasingly used
by estate agents):

| Item | Typical |
| --- | ---: |
| Buyer's reservation fee | 4.5% inc VAT, minimum around £6,600 |

The reservation fee is paid by the **buyer**, does not come off the purchase
price, and is non-refundable. On a £100,000 lot the minimum fee is 6.6% of the
price. Model it as a purchase cost and point it out explicitly — it frequently
turns a marginal deal into a bad one on its own.

## Legal and transactional

| Item | Typical |
| --- | ---: |
| Solicitor, company purchase | £1,400 – £2,200 |
| Solicitor, auction purchase (tight timescale, more work) | £1,800 – £2,800 |
| Searches | £350 – £500 |
| LTT return filing | £100 – £200 |
| Lender's legal costs on a bridge | £1,200 – £2,500 |
| Bank transfer / completion fees | £50 – £100 |
| Land Registry fee | £45 – £330 by price band |
| Survey — level 2 homebuyer | £500 – £800 |
| Survey — level 3 building survey (advisable on anything needing works) | £800 – £1,400 |

A company purchase costs more than a personal one because the solicitor has to
deal with the lender's requirements on the SPV, personal guarantees and often a
debenture.

## Refurbishment

Treat per-square-foot rules as a cross-check on a schedule of works, never as a
substitute for one.

| Level | Typical |
| --- | ---: |
| Cosmetic — decorate, flooring, tidy kitchen and bathroom | £8,000 – £15,000 |
| Standard BTL refurb — new kitchen, bathroom, carpets, decoration, some rewire | £18,000 – £30,000 |
| Full refurb — rewire, replumb, new kitchen and bathroom, plaster, boiler, windows | £30,000 – £50,000 |
| Heavy — roof, structural, damp, layout changes, extension | £50,000+ |

Add on top where relevant: new roof £7,000–£15,000; full rewire £4,000–£7,000;
new boiler and system £3,000–£5,000; damp proofing £2,000–£6,000; windows
£4,000–£8,000; loft conversion £25,000–£45,000.

**Contingency: 15% as standard, 20–25% on anything pre-1930 or with known damp,
structural or roof issues.** Older Welsh terraces routinely reveal problems once
the plaster comes off. The contingency is not padding; it is the part of the
budget you have not discovered yet.

Also budget for the EPC. A rented property currently needs at least an E, and
the direction of travel is towards C for new tenancies. Solid-wall Victorian
terraces — most of the stock in this market — are the hardest and most expensive
to lift, and an unfixable EPC is a reason to walk away rather than a cost line.

## Finance

**Bridging** (typical for auction purchases, where 28 days rules out a normal mortgage):

| Item | Typical |
| --- | ---: |
| Loan to value | 70 – 75% of purchase price |
| Interest | 0.85% – 1.2% per month |
| Arrangement fee | 2% of loan |
| Exit fee | 0 – 1% |
| Lender legal and valuation | £1,500 – £2,500 |
| Broker fee | £995 – £2,000 |

Interest is usually rolled up and repaid on exit, which means it compounds into
the redemption figure rather than hitting monthly cashflow. Model the term
generously: a refurb that slips two months adds two months of interest and the
bridge does not care about your reasons.

**BTL refinance into a limited company:**

| Item | Typical |
| --- | ---: |
| Loan to value | 75% maximum, 70 – 75% common |
| Rate | 5% – 6% on a 5-year fix |
| Product fee | 3% of loan, usually added to the loan |
| Valuation and legal | £700 – £1,200 |
| Broker fee | £500 – £1,500 |
| Interest cover requirement | 125% at the higher of pay rate + 2% or 5.5% |

The interest cover ratio is the constraint people forget. It means the loan is
capped at roughly **annual rent ÷ 0.06875** regardless of the property's value.
At £850 a month that is about £148,000. Below roughly £200,000 of end value the
LTV cap usually binds first, but on a high-value, low-yielding property the
stress test bites and the refinance releases far less than expected.

Most lenders also require **six months' ownership** before refinancing at the new
value. Plan the bridge term around that, not just around the works.

## Ongoing operating costs

| Item | Typical |
| --- | ---: |
| Letting agent, full management | 10% – 12% of rent |
| Letting agent, tenant find only | 50% – 100% of one month's rent |
| Maintenance reserve | 5% – 10% of rent |
| Voids reserve | 5% – 8% of rent |
| Landlord insurance | £300 – £450 a year |
| Gas safety, EICR, EPC amortised | £150 – £250 a year |
| Ground rent and service charge, leasehold flats | £1,000 – £2,500 a year |

Leasehold service charges deserve a hard look. On an ex-local-authority block they
can exceed £2,000 a year and carry major works bills that arrive without warning,
which is enough to destroy the cashflow on an otherwise sound flat.

## Wales-specific

**Rent Smart Wales.** Every landlord of a Welsh property must register, and
anyone letting or managing must be licensed. Registration is modest, around £40,
and a landlord licence around £150 for five years, but letting without one is a
criminal offence and blocks a section 173 possession notice.

**Renting Homes (Wales) Act 2016.** Welsh tenancies are occupation contracts, not
ASTs. Notice periods for a landlord's no-fault notice are six months, and cannot
be served in the first six months of a contract — materially longer than England.
Factor this into void assumptions and into any exit plan that depends on getting
the property back.

**Holding costs during the works.** Council tax is the one to watch. Welsh
councils may charge a premium of up to 300% on long-term empty properties, and
Conwy, Gwynedd, Denbighshire and Pembrokeshire all apply substantial premiums.
Exemptions for properties undergoing major repair are discretionary and
time-limited. Check the specific council's policy before assuming the property is
free to hold while empty — on a long refurb this can run to thousands.
